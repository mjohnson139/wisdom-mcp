## Text

In the highest antiquity, (the people) did not know that there were (their rulers).  In the next age they loved them and praised them.  In the next they feared them; in the next they despised them. Thus it was that when faith (in the Tao) was deficient (in the rulers) a want of faith in them ensued (in the people).

How irresolute did those (earliest rulers) appear, showing (by their reticence) the importance which they set upon their words! Their work was done and their undertakings were successful, while the people all said, 'We are as we are, of ourselves!'

## Reflection

The chapter ranks leaders. The best are barely known to exist. Next come those who are loved and praised, then those who are feared, and last those who are despised. When the work is done, people say, "We did it ourselves." That is not false modesty. It is the outcome of a leader who trusts people enough to let them own the work. When a leader lacks trust, people sense it and become untrustworthy in turn. Over-managing teaches people to wait to be managed. The quiet leader sets the conditions, steps back, and lets the group discover its own capability.

## Question

If my team succeeded this quarter, would they say "we did it ourselves", or would they credit (or blame) me?

## Practice

Pick one decision you would normally make yourself this week. Hand it to the team with the context and constraints, and then do not override the result.
