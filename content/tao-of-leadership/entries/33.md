## Text

He who knows other men is discerning; he who knows himself is intelligent.  He who overcomes others is strong; he who overcomes himself is mighty.  He who is satisfied with his lot is rich; he who goes on acting with energy has a (firm) will.

He who does not fail in the requirements of his position, continues long; he who dies and yet does not perish, has longevity.

## Reflection

Knowing others is useful. Knowing yourself is what makes you clear. Overcoming others takes force; overcoming yourself takes real strength. This chapter turns the leader's attention inward. Your reactions, your appetite for recognition, your fear of being wrong: these shape the room more than your strategy does. "He who is satisfied with his lot is rich." A leader who knows when they have enough, of control, of credit, of being right, can make decisions that aren't secretly about themselves.

## Question

Which of my reactions this week was really about me, and not about the work?

## Practice

At the end of each day, write one sentence about a moment you reacted, and what you were protecting in that moment.
