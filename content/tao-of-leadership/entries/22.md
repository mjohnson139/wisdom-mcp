## Text

The partial becomes complete; the crooked, straight; the empty, full; the worn out, new.  He whose (desires) are few gets them; he whose (desires) are many goes astray.

Therefore the sage holds in his embrace the one thing (of humility), and manifests it to all the world.  He is free from self-display, and therefore he shines; from self-assertion, and therefore he is distinguished; from self-boasting, and therefore his merit is acknowledged; from self-complacency, and therefore he acquires superiority.  It is because he is thus free from striving that therefore no one in the world is able to strive with him.

That saying of the ancients that 'the partial becomes complete' was not vainly spoken:--all real completion is comprehended under it.

## Reflection

Bend and you stay whole. Empty yourself and you are filled. These read like paradoxes until you've seen them work. The leader who must always be right, seen and credited becomes brittle and exhausting to follow. The leader who doesn't need to win every exchange has room to listen, and people come toward them. "It is because he is thus free from striving that therefore no one in the world is able to strive with him." Yielding here is not weakness. It is refusing to spend your energy on contests that don't matter, so you have it for the ones that do.

## Question

What am I trying to prove right now, and what would change if I stopped needing to?

## Practice

Today, when you notice the urge to correct, defend or one-up someone in a low-stakes moment, let it pass and say "tell me more" instead.
