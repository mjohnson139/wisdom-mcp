## Text

The highest excellence is like (that of) water.  The excellence of water appears in its benefiting all things, and in its occupying, without striving (to the contrary), the low place which all men dislike.  Hence (its way) is near to (that of) the Tao.

The excellence of a residence is in (the suitability of) the place; that of the mind is in abysmal stillness; that of associations is in their being with the virtuous; that of government is in its securing good order; that of (the conduct of) affairs is in its ability; and that of (the initiation of) any movement is in its timeliness.

And when (one with the highest excellence) does not wrangle (about his low position), no one finds fault with him.

## Reflection

Water does the most essential work in any landscape, and it does it from the lowest place. It doesn't argue with the rock; it goes around, and over time the rock changes shape. A leader who is like water serves first, fits the container of the moment, and stays out of contests that only feed the ego. Notice the list in this chapter: the right place, a still mind, good company, good order, ability, good timing. None of it is flashy. All of it is useful. Influence that doesn't strive is hard to resist, because there is nothing to push back against.

## Question

Where am I pushing against a rock that water would simply flow around?

## Practice

In your next difficult conversation, don't argue for your position. Ask two questions, find where the other person is actually trying to go, and look for the channel that serves both of you.
