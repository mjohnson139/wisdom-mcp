## Text

He who devotes himself to learning (seeks) from day to day to increase (his knowledge); he who devotes himself to the Tao (seeks) from day to day to diminish (his doing).

He diminishes it and again diminishes it, till he arrives at doing nothing (on purpose).  Having arrived at this point of non-action, there is nothing which he does not do.

He who gets as his own all under heaven does so by giving himself no trouble (with that end).  If one take trouble (with that end), he is not equal to getting as his own all under heaven.

## Reflection

Learning adds something every day. Practicing the Way removes something every day, until you reach not-doing, and then nothing is left undone. Experienced leaders accumulate frameworks, habits and opinions, and after a while those get in the way of seeing what is actually in front of them. This chapter asks you to subtract: fewer interventions, fewer meetings, fewer rules, fewer assumptions. What is left is attention, and attention is what the situation needs.

## Question

What could I stop doing, as a leader, that would make things work better?

## Practice

List the recurring things you do as a leader (meetings, approvals, check-ins). Cross one off for two weeks and watch what happens.
